# Portable House Data Project Background

Across Canada, the residential building stock must be rapidly retrofitted to lower emissions and energy costs and improve resilience in the face of a climate and affordability crisis. While a wide range of efforts and services exist throughout the country to help achieve these goals, they are often fragmented, occurring in silos across incompatible tools and formats. Effective communication and cooperation are critical to ensure meaningful real-world action takes place.

The Portable House Data standard was created to provide a simple, clear, machine-readable JSON structure that captures the essentials (and details when available) of residential buildings for energy analysis purposes. It is designed to allow data to be easily exchanged between assessment tools, modelling engines, utilities, municipalities, and other service providers such as Energy Advisors (EAs) or similar consultants. This can help improve data quality, reduce re-entry requirements and translation errors, and lower the cost and time required to deliver credible home energy insights.

Interoperability is the core design principle. In an effort to prevent data from being locked behind proprietary systems, this standard enables data to be exchanged across platforms and services, preserving data provenance and aligning with familiar terminology and systems (ERS/HOT2000/HPXML). This data standard is designed with the hope that it can, in the future, support the continued delivery of the EnerGuide Rating System (ERS) and the independent role of Registered Energy Advisors across Canada by making virtual asset inputs, details collected on-site, and model outputs portable, verifiable, and reusable across workflows that include HOT2000 and related tools.

By lowering friction between systems and services, this standard hopes to support an ecosystem for existing and future services that may include analytics, retrofit planning and design, quoting, permitting, program delivery, financing, and beyond. Vendors can build focused software and consulting service offerings without rebuilding data models from scratch. Homeowners, contractors, and program administrators can benefit from transparent, comparable data. Additionally, this data standard can help lower the barriers to entry for new players in the market, accelerating innovation while maintaining accuracy, accountability, and consumer protection.

The work encompassing the first version of the portable house data standard was made possible by funding provided by Alberta Ecotrust. The work was completed by Volta Research, with support from Lightspark. We hope the community of contributors to this work broadens as we continue our efforts towards helping Canada achieve its low-carbon, low-cost building goals.
